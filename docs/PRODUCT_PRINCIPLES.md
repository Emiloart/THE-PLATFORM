# Product Principles

These principles guide future product decisions. They record current direction and should be amended deliberately rather than silently overwritten.

## 1. One coherent platform, dedicated experiences

The product should feel integrated, but each major activity should have an interface designed for its purpose. Do not collapse text, video, audio, commerce, work, and events into one undifferentiated feed.

## 2. No unnecessary exits

A user should be able to move between social, media, commerce, services, work, events, and payments without repeatedly logging out or switching to unrelated applications.

## 3. Integration is not indiscriminate data sharing

Shared accounts and navigation do not justify unrestricted data access. Identity, private messages, financial records, viewing behaviour, business records, and other sensitive domains require explicit boundaries and least-privilege access.

## 4. Trust must be operational

Verification is one control, not the entire trust system. The platform must also address authenticity, quality, fulfilment, service outcomes, fraud, abuse, refunds, disputes, appeals, and accountability.

## 5. One individual account; independent organisation accounts

Each individual has one primary platform account. Do not multiply personal profiles merely to represent creator, professional, consumer, or other activities. Organisations have independent accounts and can add individuals through HDIP-established relationships, then assign scoped platform permissions. Individuals may belong to multiple organisations. Users can switch into an organisation context they are authorised to represent, and the active identity must always be clear. Organisations may have multiple HDIP-established authorised owners, supported by explicit ownership, recovery, transfer, and succession controls.

## 6. Preserve role distinctions

Individuals, creators, merchants, restaurants, drivers, professionals, and organisations have different workflows and assurance requirements. Represent these differences through verified claims, organisation membership, context, and scoped permissions rather than requiring multiple personal accounts.

## 7. Monetisation should not depend universally on audience size

The intended model allows eligible verified participants to begin monetising from the point they meet the required verification and applicable rules. Do not interpret this as guaranteed income or automatic access to every revenue mechanism.

## 8. Multiple revenue engines

Advertising and subscriptions are not the sole business model. Commerce, services, fulfilment, creator tools, ticketing, and other value-added activities may contribute revenue.

## 9. Transparent economics

Commissions, service fees, delivery charges, subscriptions, refunds, and settlement timing must be understandable before a user commits to a transaction.

## 10. Build for global operation, validate market by market

The product ambition is global, but payments, financial services, transportation, delivery, identity checks, consumer protection, privacy, and licensing requirements vary by jurisdiction.

## 11. Identity integration and assurance

Identity credentials can establish verified attributes, but they do not prove conduct, competence, product authenticity, or service quality. Apply role-specific assurance and maintain separate reputation, monitoring, review, and enforcement processes.

## 12. Preserve ideas before prioritising

The feature catalogue is a long-term capability inventory. Recording a feature does not mean it belongs in the first release. Do not delete or narrow ideas merely to make an early roadmap appear smaller.

## 13. Distinguish facts, proposals, and decisions

Documents should label confirmed direction, proposed capabilities, assumptions, unresolved questions, and decisions. Do not present an inference as a founder-approved requirement.

## 14. Design for operational reality

Every customer-facing capability implies support, moderation, dispute handling, fraud controls, reliability, observability, and internal tooling. Include these in feature specifications.

## 15. AI assists; policy governs

AI may support discovery, moderation, customer service, translation, creator workflows, and operational analysis. High-impact actions involving identity, money, enforcement, or access require explicit policies, auditability, and appropriate human review.

## 16. Separate product decisions from technical approval

Product direction and technical implementation decisions must be recorded independently. A product requirement does not automatically approve a particular framework, vendor, database topology, or deployment architecture. Technical choices must be justified against the requirements and validated before they become binding decisions.



## Age-aware access and guardianship

Kids Mode and age-appropriate access are based on the user's HDIP-established age grade. HDIP establishes relevant age and guardian relationships; the platform applies section-specific access rules and privileges assigned by established parents or guardians. Age policies must be scoped by activity and reviewed against the law in each operating market.

## Configurable navigation and context preservation

Provide persistent primary navigation that users can arrange and prioritise. Global search spans the platform and supports destination-specific filters. Transitions between dedicated experiences should preserve useful context, including returning users to the originating social post after they open an embedded video preview.

## Background activity and dedicated modes

Music and audio playback should continue while users navigate elsewhere, with clear pause and stop controls. Video should support a dedicated offline-viewing mode that can switch off other platform activities. Other background behaviours must be defined by activity and user control.

## Progressive feature enablement

Keep initial onboarding and the core installation lean. Optional experience modules and role-specific tools should be installable or enabled when users need them. This is a product requirement, not a mandated delivery mechanism: implementation must respect operating-system, app-store, security, and compatibility constraints. Optional modules must use shared identity and policy controls and must not fragment the account model.


## Media capabilities are architectural requirements

Background audio, offline video, live streaming, synchronised watch experiences, media-session controls, downloads, playback continuity, and platform-specific lifecycle behaviour must be treated as first-class architectural requirements. Do not assume a cross-platform UI framework can satisfy these requirements without measurable validation. Native platform capabilities and failure behaviour must be evaluated before approving the mobile application architecture.
