# Product Vision

## Status

Working product vision, version 0.1. This document records the current direction; it is not a final business plan, technical architecture, or release commitment.

## The ambition

Build an integrated digital environment in which people can spend meaningful portions of their digital lives without repeatedly leaving to use unrelated services. People should be able to socialise, publish and consume media, discover products, buy and sell, order food, book transport and services, arrange appointments, work together, attend events, and transact within one connected platform.

The ambition is broader than a social network and broader than a conventional marketplace. It combines consumer experiences, creator tools, business infrastructure, on-demand services, and financial capabilities.

## Product experience

The platform should be experienced as one coherent product with multiple dedicated destinations. Integration should not mean forcing every activity into one feed or one interface.

Examples:
- Text conversations and social publishing belong in a social environment.
- Long-form video and short clips belong in a video-first environment.
- Music, podcasts, and audiobooks need a purpose-built audio experience.
- Shopping needs structured catalogues, comparison, checkout, fulfilment, and buyer protection.
- Meetings and conferences need collaboration controls rather than social-feed mechanics.

Users should move between these environments without repeatedly logging out, creating unrelated accounts, or losing relevant context.

## Marketplace thesis

The marketplace is not intended to be an open classifieds board where anyone can list anything without meaningful checks. The intended model is a managed, verified commercial environment drawing inspiration from large product marketplaces and commerce platforms, while developing its own operating model.

The platform may act as an intermediary in transactions and may coordinate or integrate payment, fulfilment, delivery, buyer protection, seller protection, refunds, and dispute resolution. The exact legal and operational role must be defined by market and transaction type.

Commerce extends beyond physical products:
- Restaurants can list menus and accept orders.
- Drivers and mobility providers can offer transport.
- Professionals and local providers can list bookable services.
- Customers can discover, compare, book, pay, and review within the platform.

Trust cannot rely on identity verification alone. Merchant quality, fulfilment performance, product authenticity, service outcomes, complaints, and dispute history need separate controls.

## Creator and media thesis

The platform should provide distinct environments for written social content, video, music, podcasts, audiobooks, and live broadcasts. Creators should have tools to publish, manage audiences, understand performance, and earn through multiple eligible methods.

The product direction is to make monetisation eligibility available from the point of successful required verification, rather than imposing arbitrary audience-size thresholds as the universal prerequisite. Eligibility is not guaranteed income. Each monetisation method remains subject to relevant content, rights, policy, legal, safety, and payment requirements.

## Meetings, events, and shared experiences

The platform should support productive and communal activities in addition to passive content consumption:
- One-to-one and group meetings.
- Appointments and consultations.
- Team collaboration.
- Virtual conferences and speaker sessions.
- Movie parties and synchronised viewing, subject to content licensing and access rights.
- Listening parties, watch parties, and community gatherings.
- Ticketing, registration, invitations, and attendance management.

## Commercial model

The business should not depend exclusively on advertising or premium subscriptions. Potential revenue sources include:
- Marketplace commissions.
- Food-order and service-booking commissions.
- Transaction and payment-related fees where lawful and justified.
- Delivery and fulfilment economics.
- Creator tools and optional paid services.
- Advertising and sponsored placements.
- Subscriptions and memberships.
- Event ticketing and paid experiences.
- Business tools and value-added services.

Pricing must be transparent. Convenience fees or commissions should correspond to a clear service or value and must not undermine trust.

## Identity and verification

HDIP (Hybrid Decentralised Identity Passport) is a separate project and may serve as an identity and credential verification foundation for this platform.

The intended direction is to require HDIP-backed verification for verified status and monetisation eligibility. Different roles may require additional credentials: an individual, creator, merchant, restaurant, driver, professional, or organisation should not all be treated as identical verification cases.

Identity proof is not a guarantee of conduct, product quality, professional competence, or successful fulfilment. The platform needs separate reputation, monitoring, moderation, and enforcement systems.

## Product boundaries

- This platform is independent of ShadeFast. ShadeFast remains a separate product with its own identity, architecture, purpose, audience, and business model.
- HDIP remains a separate identity infrastructure project. The platform may integrate with it without owning or redefining it.
- “THE-PLATFORM” is only a working repository label. The final product name and brand are undecided.

## Current stage

The immediate work is product discovery and definition: preserve the idea set, clarify the experience domains, document workflows and business rules, identify dependencies and risks, and only then develop a technical architecture and staged delivery plan.
